Luxury is a Point of View

Tamara Kaye-Honey

Author

Tamara Kaye-Honey

D

ear Honey, how do I make my home feel more luxurious without spending a fortune?

Luxury isn't always a price tag... we like to think of it as a point of view.

The homes that leave a lasting impression aren't necessarily the ones with the biggest budgets. They're the ones that feel thoughtful, layered, and deeply personal. Here's where we'd invest our energy... and where we'd happily save our money.

Get the lighting right.
Nothing gives away a room faster than bad lighting. Start by thinking beyond the overhead fixture... lamps, sconces, picture lights, pendants. Great rooms have light coming from more than one direction and, ideally - at more than one height. And put everything on a dimmer. Everything.

Change the hardware.
Consider it jewelry for the room. Cabinet pulls, knobs, switch plates, door hardware... these are relatively small details that have an outsized effect. They also don't need to match. In fact, we'd prefer they didn't. Tension keeps things interesting.

Buy less. Buy better.
You do not need to furnish an entire room at once. We would much rather see one incredible chair sitting next to a table you found at a flea market than a room full of furniture purchased from the same showroom. Save for the pieces that matter... let the rest find you.

Hang the curtains higher.
It's one of the oldest tricks in the book because it works... take the drapery as high as the architecture allows and let it properly meet the floor. Suddenly the windows feel taller and the ceilings feel higher.

Give the walls something to say.
Paint is one of the least expensive ways to completely change the architecture of a room. Don't be afraid to take it beyond the walls... paint the trim, the ceiling, the doors. Or.... wrap the entire room in one color. Commitment looks confident.
Scale up.
One of the quickest ways to make a room feel underwhelming is filling it with too many small things. A larger piece of art... a generously scaled lamp... one substantial bowl instead of six tiny objects having a meeting on your coffee table. When in doubt, fewer and bigger.

Mix high and low.
This is where rooms start to develop personality... put the vintage chair next to the custom sofa. Hang the inexpensive piece of art in a beautiful frame. Pair the investment dining table with chairs that didn't require a second mortgage. If everything in a room comes from the same price point... or the same decade - it tends to show.

Look down.
A rug should anchor the room, not float nervously in the middle of it. Go larger than you think. At minimum, the front legs of your furniture should comfortably sit on it. An undersized rug has an uncanny ability to make everything around it look smaller too....... we don't make the rules.

Edit... then edit again.
Luxury needs breathing room. Not every wall needs art, not every surface needs an object, not every corner needs a chair. Once you've finished a room, take something away... and maybe one more thing.

Finally, make it yours.
The most luxurious homes don't look like anyone else's. Keep the strange thing you bought on vacation. Frame the drawing your child made. Reupholster your grandmother's chair in something she absolutely would not have chosen. Buy the art you can't stop thinking about. A home should reveal something about the people who live there... otherwise, it's just a very nice room.

I guess... spend where you'll feel it. Save where you won't.

The most luxurious homes don't look like anyone else's.

Tamara Kaye-Honey
