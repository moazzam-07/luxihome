Tackling Kolkata’s Wall Dampness (Nona) Before Painting: The Permanent 3-Step Fix

Nothing breaks a homeowner’s heart faster than watching ₹50,000 worth of luxury velvet-finish paint bubble, crack, and turn to white salt powder within three months of moving in. In Kolkata, efflorescence—locally known as nona—is a structural epidemic.

Executive Takeaways

  • •Nona is caused by dissolved mineral salts travelling through porous brickwork and crystallizing as moisture evaporates.
  • •Applying waterproofing paint directly over peeling putty is a temporary 3-month band-aid.
  • •Permanent treatment requires chipping the plaster down to the bare brick and establishing a chemical barrier.
  • •Always test internal moisture percentages with an electronic pin-type moisture meter before applying primer.

1. Why Traditional Putty and Primer Fail

Kolkata is built on alluvial delta soil with an exceptionally high sub-soil water table. In ground-floor, first-floor, and shaft-adjacent walls, moisture climbs vertically through capillary action.

When painters scrape off peeling paint and instantly apply acrylic wall putty, they trap moisture behind a vapor barrier. As summer heat warms the room, water vapor pressure blows the paint right off the wall.

2. The Permanent 3-Step Remediation Protocol

Step 1: Scrape the plaster 1 foot above the damp zone down to the red brickwork. Treat the exposed bricks with a salt-neutralizing chemical wash.

Step 2: Apply a two-component crystalline polymer waterproof slurry (such as Dr. Fixit Dampguard or Fosroc Brushbond). The active chemicals migrate deep into the brick pores, forming insoluble crystals that permanently block capillary micro-pores.

Step 3: Re-plaster using sand and cement mixed with an integral waterproofing liquid compound. Allow 14 days of complete curing before painting.

Insider Trade Secret

The Hidden Shaft Wall Moisture Trap

In 90% of Kolkata apartments, dampness in master bedroom wardrobes originates from the adjacent plumbing duct. Before installing any built-in wardrobe against a shaft wall, leave a 2-inch ventilated air gap and screw an exterior-grade cement fiber board (Shera board) behind the wardrobe carcass. Never let timber touch raw brick.
